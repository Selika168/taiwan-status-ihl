# 05 — Lawful bases for the rights of the people of the territory

## Core fact: no option of nationality was ever given
| Date | Act | Option |
|---|---|---|
| 1895 | Shimonoseki Art. 5 | yes (2 years) |
| Dec 1945 | Election Law amendment suspends franchise of 外地 register holders [verify article] | none |
| 1947-05-02 | Alien Registration Ordinance (Imperial Ordinance No. 207) deems Formosans aliens "for the time being" — one day before the popular-sovereignty Constitution | none |
| 1946-01-12 | ROC order (occupant) confers nationality | none in Taiwan; abroad yes |
| 1952-04 | Circular 民事甲第438號 — loss of Japanese nationality | none |
Japan, invoking popular sovereignty, renounced Taiwan while excluding Taiwanese — still Japanese nationals — from that "people".

## Basis 1 — Occupation law (custom; applicable from 1945)
- Hague Arts. 43, 45 (P09). Collective, option-less conferral of the occupant's nationality functions as imposed allegiance.
- GC IV Art. 47 (P11): protected persons "shall not be deprived, in any case or in any manner whatsoever, of the benefits of the present Convention by any change introduced, as the result of the occupation … into the institutions or government … nor by any annexation". A fortiori: if annexation cannot remove protection, a nationality decree cannot. Pictet p. 276: such actions "would have no effect on the rights of protected persons". Art. 6(3): Art. 47 binds for the duration of occupation while the occupant exercises governmental functions.
- Pictet p. 274: Art. 47 itself "does not expressly prohibit" changes — the prohibition comes from general international law; Art. 47 guarantees rights notwithstanding.
- ICJ Wall (P74) para. 95: Art. 2(2) "is not to restrict the scope of application … by excluding therefrom territories not falling under the sovereignty of one of the contracting parties"; protection "regardless of the status of the occupied territories, as is shown by Article 47"; para. 101 "no need for any enquiry into the precise prior status". → answers the "missing reversioner" objection.
- Limits: GC IV in force 1950; ROC signed 1949, never ratified; PRC ratified 1956 (in force 1957) [R: ICRC timeline; WIPO Lex]; common Art. 2(3) needs at least one Party to the conflict. Wall applied GC IV because both States were parties when the 1967 conflict broke out; Taiwan's occupation began in 1945 → use customary Hague law by the same purposive logic.
- Dilemma for opponents: treaties bind States, not governments; if the ROC is "China" (its own and the PRC's claim), its forces carry China's GC obligations since 1957; if it is not China, it is a foreign authority holding territory it entered for the Allies, bound by occupation law as custom. Either horn binds.
- **No selective compliance.** Obligations are owed to protected persons, not to the enemy, and are not reciprocal: GC IV Art. 1 "to respect and to ensure respect for the present Convention in all circumstances"; Art. 8 "Protected persons may in no circumstances renounce in part or in entirety the rights secured to them"; VCLT Art. 60(5) (P18): breach-suspension rules "do not apply to provisions relating to the protection of the human person contained in treaties of a humanitarian character"; Kupreškić (ICTY 2000) paras 517–518 rejects tu quoque [verify]. An authority cannot invoke the Conventions when attacked while disregarding them toward the inhabitants it controls; the customary basis it accepts (P91) has no territorial carve-out. "Accepts and applies" (common Art. 2(3)) means applying in fact, everywhere.
- **If the ROC is treated as an exile government / dissident force of China:** China's ratification binds every party to a conflict on Chinese territory, armed groups included (Common Art. 3 "each Party to the conflict shall be bound"; AP II); in Taiwan — not Chinese territory on the chain — it holds territory as occupant (custom). Both tracks bind it toward the inhabitants. Non-State commitments (Common Art. 3(3) special agreements; AP I Art. 96(3) declarations; Geneva Call deeds) give practical effect; none has been lodged by the ROC.
- But an ROC–PRC war between two governments both claiming to be China is **non-international** (Common Art. 3, AP II, custom) — occupation rules reach Taiwan only on the premise that Taiwan is not Chinese territory. On that premise, any future PRC control of Taiwan would also be an occupation, with GC IV duties toward inhabitants who are not its nationals.

### How GC IV can apply to renounced, untransferred, continuously occupied territory (routes, most to least robust)
- **R1 Custom (no Art. 2 problem).** Hague Regs. 42–56 are customary (Wall para. 89 [verify — extract has 90–101]); core GC IV protections (humane treatment, no deportation/transfer, no compelled service) are customary (ICRC Customary IHL Study [verify rule numbers]). **The administering authority's own military doctrine accepts this premise** (P91, MND Air Force Academic Bimonthly no. 640, 2014): the LOAC treaties 「已由其多邊立法性而形成國際習慣法，對於非締約國亦具有強制的拘束效力；因此，我國雖未簽署「武裝衝突法」的條約，也須受其約束而遵行」; and 「我國現今雖然不再是聯合國的會員國，未簽署這些國際條約，但仍有主動遵守相關國際法的意願」; MND issued ICRC-based "rules of conduct in military operations" cards to all troops in March 2006. Use: the authority cannot deny being bound by customary LOAC — including the customary occupation rules (the article itself addresses conduct of hostilities; its premise covers the whole customary body). Weight: a service-journal article published by the MND, evidence of doctrine, not a formal declaration. Its 「未簽署」 is wrong: the ROC signed in 1949 and never ratified. Weakness: the treaty status "protected person" and treaty machinery (Protecting Power, Art. 143 visits) are not fully customary.
- **R2 Treaty via Art. 2(1).** P11: "all cases of declared war or of any other armed conflict which may arise between two or more of the High Contracting Parties, **even if the state of war is not recognized by one of them**." China declared war on Japan on 9 Dec 1941 [verify source]; Japan a party from 1953, China (PRC ratification) from 1957-06-28. The PRC rejects the Treaty of Taipei, and the 1972 Joint Communiqué preamble (P76), signed by both, speaks of "the termination of the state of war" (戦争状態の終結／战争状态的结束). So on the view of the government the UN recognises as China's only legitimate representative, a declared state of war between two parties existed 1957–72; Japan's view (ended 1952) is irrelevant under "not recognized by one of them". Taiwan was occupied in the course of that war; Wall para. 101: "no need for any enquiry into the precise prior status". Continuation: GC IV Art. 6(3) — Arts. 1–12, 27, 29–34, 47, 49, 51, 52, 53, 59, 61–77, 143 bind "for the duration of the occupation, to the extent that such Power exercises the functions of government"; AP I Art. 3(b) (P19) — for AP I parties the Conventions cease "in the case of occupied territories, on the termination of the occupation" (China an AP I party [verify date]). Use: estoppel against the PRC above all; the ROC's own view (war ended 1952, before China was bound) does not yield it. Weaknesses: whether a state of war without hostilities is "declared war" (Pictet on Art. 2 [check]); the PRC's "restored in 1945" reply (answer: Pictet p. 275; no PRC–Japan settlement until 1972/1978, after Japan had renounced); Wall's "when the conflict broke out" — fact or requirement (VCLT 28 continuing situation); the occupant would be China, so Art. 4 nationality exclusion returns (see Basis 2).
- **R3 Art. 2(2) alone** — Taiwan was Japan's when occupied, Japan became a party in 1953 after renouncing; weak alone, secondary after Wall para. 95.
- **R4 Unilateral undertaking.** P91 shows a stated *willingness* to comply (「主動遵守相關國際法的意願」) — a starting point for asking for a formal declaration of de facto application in Formosa and the Pescadores. The depositary accepted Palestine's 1982 unilateral undertaking (Wall para. 91); Israel declared de facto application of "humanitarian provisions" (para. 93). A request to the administering authority to declare de facto application in Formosa and the Pescadores builds the record even if refused.
- **R5 Authoritative qualification.** Occupation is a fact the ICRC qualifies itself; a UNGA request for an ICJ advisory opinion would settle applicability, as in Wall.
- **Safety net:** ICCPR/ICESCR apply to persons under jurisdiction, including in occupied territory (Wall paras 106–111 [verify]); the ROC domesticated both Covenants (2009 Implementation Act [verify]). Human-rights communications can run even if GC IV applicability is contested.

### Daily-life acts are not allegiance
- **Conscription:** GC IV Art. 51 — "The Occupying Power may not compel protected persons to serve in its armed or auxiliary forces. No pressure or propaganda which aims at securing voluntary enlistment is permitted." Compelled service is the occupant's breach; it cannot be turned into evidence of allegiance. Volunteers: while serving they fall under GC I–III (excluded from Art. 4 for that period); under the Tadić test voluntary career service weakens an individual claim but is no treaty bar.
- **Documents:** using the only documents an administration issues is necessity — Okinawa under US administration (travel documents issued by the US civil administration while Japan kept residual sovereignty, SFPT Art. 3); Namibia AO para. 125 [verify]. Weak spot: actively seeking the administration's consular protection abroad (Tadić's diplomatic-protection criterion).
- **Elections:** no court or treaty body has held that voting under occupation forfeits protected status; self-determination ends in a vote. Do not advise abstention as a legal strategy; advise a written, signed reservation that participation in elections, use of documents, taxes and military service arise from necessity or compulsion, do not express allegiance and waive no GC IV or nationality-option claim — it also records non-acquiescence against prescription.

## Basis 2 — Nationality and human-rights law
- UDHR Art. 15 (P42); ICCPR Arts. 2, 24(3), 26 (P16); ICERD Art. 5(d)(iii); A/RES/55/153 annex Arts. 4, 5, 11, 16 (P59) as evidence of the standard (not retroactive).
- Const. of Japan Art. 10; Iwasawa (1998) [verify].
- Protected-person status by substance: Tadić (P72) para. 166 "substantial relations more than formal bonds"; para. 168 a later Citizenship Act does not alter; para. 169 "even if … the perpetrators and the victims were to be regarded as possessing the same nationality, Article 4 would still be applicable." Caveat: substance cuts both ways (present-day allegiance).
- Stateless route: if the occupant's conferral is not opposable and Japan withdrew its nationality, the persons are stateless — GC IV Art. 4's negative definition covers stateless persons (Pictet p. 47; Tadić para. 164).
- Namibia AO (1971) para. 125 [verify wording]: acts of even an unlawful administration such as civil registration should not be ignored to the detriment of inhabitants → using ROC records concedes no sovereignty.

## Basis 3 — Self-determination
- Atlantic Charter (1941) point 2; UN Charter Art. 1(2) (P07); UNGA 2625 (P55): "the establishment of a sovereign and independent State, the free association or integration with an independent State or the emergence into any other political status freely determined by a people".
- Chagos AO 2019 (P73): para. 160 "any detachment … unless based on the freely expressed and genuine will of the people of the territory concerned, is contrary to the right to self-determination"; para. 172 "it is not possible to talk of an international agreement, when one of the parties … was under the authority of the latter" and "heightened scrutiny … to the issue of consent"; para. 177 "an unlawful act of a continuing character"; para. 180 erga omnes.
- Limits: customary rule anchored in Res. 1514 (1960); Mauritius was an NSGT from 1946 (para. 144); Taiwan never listed.
