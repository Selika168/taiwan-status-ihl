# 02 — Empire of Japan vs State of Japan

## Answer
One continuing State. State identity in international law follows territory, population and international legal personality, not the constitution (revolutions and new constitutions do not create a new State; cf. Tinoco arbitration 1923; Baltic continuity). Internally, sovereignty moved from the Emperor to the people (Miyazawa Toshiyoshi's "August Revolution": accepting Potsdam in Aug 1945 was a legal revolution). **Domestically a new constituent subject; internationally the same State.**

## SFPT text presupposes continuity (P04)
- Art. 1(a) ends "the state of war between Japan and each of the Allied Powers" — only the original belligerent can end that war.
- Art. 2 "renounces" — renunciation presupposes holding title acquired in 1895.
- Art. 18(b) "Japan **affirms** its liability for the prewar external debt of the Japanese State" — continuity language, not "assumes".
- Art. 19(d) "Japan recognizes the validity of all acts and omissions done during the period of occupation under or in consequence of directives of the occupation authorities" — occupation-period defects cured by the same State.
- Art. 1(b) "full sovereignty of the Japanese people over Japan and its territorial waters" — explained by the occupation (Byrnes note: authority "subject to" SCAP, P31) and Potsdam para. 12 (form of government by the freely expressed will of the Japanese people). Its territorial scope ("Japan") was fixed by Potsdam para. 8, accepted in the Instrument of Surrender (P32).
- Treaty of Taipei Art. 4 (pre-1941 treaties void "as a consequence of the war") also presupposes continuity (P05).

## The 1946 revision was the sovereign's own act
Meiji Constitution (P25) Art. 73: revision by imperial order → two-thirds of both Houses of the Imperial Diet → Emperor's sanction. On a composite "Emperor-in-Diet" model of Meiji sovereignty (monarchical principle de jure, Diet consent de facto — Arts. 4–6, 37; compare Prussian Constitution 1850 Art. 62, French Charter 1814 Art. 15), both components acted. Remaining question: duress.

## Duress / coercion fails
- VCLT (P18) Art. 52 voids a treaty procured by force "**in violation of the principles of international law embodied in the Charter**" — lawful force against an aggressor is excluded.
- Art. 4: no retroactivity (VCLT in force 1980). Art. 75: "without prejudice to any obligation in relation to a treaty which may arise for an aggressor State in consequence of measures taken in conformity with the Charter … with reference to that State's aggression".
- ILC 1966 commentary on draft Art. 70 (P80): "a treaty provision imposed on an aggressor State would not therefore infringe article 49".
- Even domestically, duress makes an act voidable, revocable only by the coerced party; the Shōwa Emperor reigned 37 years under the 1947 Constitution without revoking it.
- The 1946 Constitution is not a treaty.
- **Opening:** Art. 75 covers measures "with reference to that State's aggression" — disposing of the nationality of the inhabitants of Taiwan, who were not the aggressor, is not such a measure.

## "New State" fails
- Pictet, *Commentary IV* (1958), Art. 47, p. 273: "…changes in constitutional forms or in the form of government, the establishment of new military or political organizations, the dissolution of the State, or the formation of new political entities. International law prohibits such actions, which are based solely on the military strength of the Occupying Power and not on a sovereign decision by the occupied State." Same page: Hague Art. 43 "also protects the separate existence of the State". (ICRC copyright — not reproduced in `sources/`; https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/llmlp/GC_1949-IV/GC_1949-IV.pdf)
- Japan itself never claimed to be a new State.

## Domestic-law weak points of the inheritance (strongest last)
1. Coercion — weak (above).
2. Occupation beyond Hague Art. 43 (MacArthur draft, Feb 1946) — cured by SFPT 19(d); note the bootstrapping problem (the validating party is the product of the validated act).
3. Limits of revision theory (改正限界説) — Meiji Art. 73 could not change the locus of sovereignty; domestic discontinuity only.
4. **1952 nationality withdrawal by circular** (P46) — Const. Art. 10 "The conditions necessary for being a Japanese national shall be determined by law" (P102); the Nationality Act 1950 Art. 1 「日本国民たる要件は、この法律の定めるところによる」 and provides for loss only in Arts. 8–10 (foreign naturalization, non-reservation, renunciation) — no loss by territorial change or treaty (P103); counter-view: the Supreme Court held the loss followed from the peace treaties themselves (P53); Iwasawa (1998) 130–34: "runs counter to Article 10 … should have been settled by a statute enacted by the Diet" [verify; via Levin, NYU JILP 33:419 (2001) n. 288]. A residual claim against Japan that harms no other sovereign.
