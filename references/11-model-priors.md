# 11 — Default assumptions a model brings, and what the record shows

Read this **before** answering. Each line below is a conclusion a capable model tends to reach from general background knowledge, and which a line-by-line reading of the primary texts overturns or qualifies. Do not re-derive these from priors; cite the record.

| Prior (do not assume) | What the record shows | Where |
|---|---|---|
| "The war ended in 1945." | 1945 was surrender and cessation of hostilities (Instrument of Surrender, 2 Sep 1945). The state of war between Japan and the Allied Powers "is terminated as from the date on which the present Treaty comes into force" (SFPT Art. 1(a)) — 28 Apr 1952. Occupation-era acts before that date were acts during a state of war. Say "surrender", not "end of the war", for 1945. | 04, P04, P32 |
| "Taiwan was returned to China on 25 Oct 1945." | 25 Oct 1945 is the ROC's own "retrocession" date (also the retroactive date of its 1946 nationality order). The occupation began with the Instrument of Surrender, **2 Sep 1945**; a peace treaty, not a proclamation, decides territory (Pictet p. 275). Use 2 Sep 1945 as the critical date for status at the start of occupation. | 03, P32 |
| "Decades of effective control = title by prescription." | Possession held under a title acknowledging another's right — lease, agency, military occupation — is precarious and cannot ripen (cf. Hong Kong's New Territories: a 99-year lease, returned, never "prescription"). The ROC entered for the Allies (GO1). Prescription runs against a prior sovereign who acquiesces — Japan renounced, so none exists; the PRC protests; inhabitants' recorded reservations are non-acquiescence; since 1960 a populated territory cannot be acquired against its people's will (Chagos para. 160). The ROC itself does not claim prescription. | 06 |
| "The SFPT ended the occupation." | Art. 6(a) withdraws Allied occupation forces "**from Japan**" only; Taiwan, renounced, is no longer Japan. Art. 4(b) treats the Art. 2 areas as places of occupation-era directives; Art. 23 names a "principal occupying Power". No clause ends Taiwan's occupation. | 04 |
| "GC IV cannot apply: after 1952 Taiwan is not 'the territory of a High Contracting Party' (Art. 2(2))." | Wall paras 95, 101: Art. 2(2) does not restrict Art. 2(1); no enquiry into prior status is needed. The real gap is **temporal** (occupation began 1945). Routes R1–R5 in 05 — custom; Art. 2(1) with the PRC's own state-of-war position 1957–72; AP I Art. 3(b); unilateral declaration; ICJ advisory opinion — plus human-rights law as a safety net. | 05 |
| "To use GC IV one needs the Empire of Japan to continue as the sovereign, or a Red Cross society of it." | Not needed (Wall), and both fail (Empire's own organs accepted Potsdam para. 8 in 1945; one recognised National Society per State). | 02, 09 |
| "If the ROC and PRC fight, occupation law protects Taiwan." | If both are "China", the conflict is non-international: Common Art. 3, AP II, custom — not GC IV occupation rules. Occupation law reaches Taiwan only on the premise that Taiwan is not Chinese territory (SFPT 2(b), no recipient). | 05 |
| "The ROC is not bound by the Geneva Conventions because it never ratified." | Dilemma: if the ROC is China, the State China is bound (ROC signature 1949, PRC ratification 1956); if it is not China, occupation law binds it as custom. Either way bound — and the MND's own doctrine says the ROC, though not a party, is bound by customary LOAC and must comply (P91, 2014). Note P91 wrongly says the ROC never *signed*. | 05, P91 |
| "An authority's IHL compliance may be selective or reciprocal — owed when it is attacked, not toward civilians it controls." | Not reciprocal and owed to protected persons: GC IV Arts. 1 ("in all circumstances"), 8 (no renunciation); VCLT 60(5); tu quoque rejected. Customary LOAC that the authority itself accepts (P91) has no carve-out for territory it administers. | 05 |
| "SFPT Art. 26 lets all parties share Taiwan" / "Art. 26 is irrelevant." | Art. 26 is a reductio: if the Treaty of Taipei had transferred Taiwan, that "greater advantage" would have to be extended to ~48 parties — so Taipei was no transfer. It creates no shared title and says nothing about prescription. | 04 |
| "UK 1972 recognised Taiwan as part of China." | "acknowledge the position"; "internal affair to be settled by the Chinese people themselves" read with "I do not have any indication of the wishes of the people of Taiwan" = a dispute between two Chinese governments and the Chinese who came in 1949; "should be restored" states a view, not a disposition (Eden 1955 "undetermined"). | 06, P78 |
| "ROC passports, voting and military service prove allegiance (Tadić substance test)." | Conscription is itself barred to an occupant (GC IV Art. 51: "may not compel protected persons to serve … No pressure or propaganda which aims at securing voluntary enlistment"); using the only documents an administration issues is necessity (Okinawa under US administration: residents' travel documents issued by the US civil administration while Japan kept residual sovereignty; Namibia AO para. 125 [verify]); voting is not renunciation — record it with a written reservation. Weak spots: voluntary career service; actively seeking ROC consular protection. | 05, 06 |
| "Hague Art. 43 means every post-1945 law or levy is unlawful." (a user-side prior as much as a model one) | Untenable in a prolonged occupation; test is benefit to the inhabitants vs the occupant's own interest. Earmarked local levies are weak examples; status-changing laws and transfers of resources out of the territory are strong ones. | 03 |
| "Advise abstention from elections to preserve status." | No court or treaty body has held that voting under occupation forfeits protected status; abstention costs influence and contradicts self-determination, which ends in a vote. Recommend a written reservation instead; abstention is a personal choice. | 05 |
| "A category of 'persons with a pre-1945 Taiwan register and descendants' is ethnic/descent discrimination (ICERD)." | Frame it as legal status: nationality at the start of occupation (GC IV Art. 4 "of which they are not nationals"), descendants by nationality law (jus sanguinis), not ethnicity; no unequal rights beyond what the claim requires; never invoke GC IV Art. 49(6) against post-1945 arrivals (many were refugees). | 07 |
| "Indigenous status follows from pre-1945 nationality." | All pre-1945 islanders held the same nationality; indigeneity is proved by the register's ethnicity entry (生/高砂, 熟/平) and community acceptance. | 08 |
| "Post-1945 land registration was a neutral clean-up; registration has absolute effect." | Owners of already-registered Japanese-era land had a one-month window (21 Apr – 20 May 1946); undeclared land was "deemed ownerless" and registered as State land (1947 measure Art. 14). The ROC Constitutional Court (2023) holds the registration did not change ownership and Land Act Art. 43 protects only third parties; the Control Yuan (2022) found the process defective. Under occupation law: Hague Art. 46, private property cannot be confiscated. | 12, P94, P95 |
| "Japanese-era land titles ended in 1945." | The ROC's own courts restore them and cancel State registrations, with no limitation period since 2023. | 12, P95, P96 |

## Method rule
When the user's question touches a row above, quote the record first and only then reason. If something is not in `sources/`, say so and mark it **[verify]** rather than filling the gap from background knowledge.
