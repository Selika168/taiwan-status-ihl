# 04 — Close reading: SFPT, Treaty of Taipei, MDT, 1972 instruments

## SFPT (P04; authentic: English, French, Spanish, Japanese)
- **Art. 2(a)** "Japan, recognizing the independence of Korea, renounces …" — participial phrase of attendant circumstance, not a condition; recognizes *independence*. **Art. 2(b)** renounces Formosa and the Pescadores — **no recipient**. The asymmetry (recipient for Korea, none for Taiwan) is the textual core.
- **Art. 3** (Ryukyus etc.): no renunciation; US administration; Japan's "residual sovereignty" (Dulles 1951). Hence Okinawa could revert; Taiwan (Art. 2) has no residue — this is why *Lin v. US* (P60, P61) could not answer "occupied territory of whom?".
- **Art. 14(a)2**: Allied right to seize Japanese property "subject to its **jurisdiction**" — jurisdiction, not sovereignty.
- **Art. 21**: "China shall be entitled to the benefits of Articles 10 and 14(a)2; and Korea to the benefits of Articles 2, 4, 9 and 12." (The unofficial Chinese text omits Art. 4 for Korea — cite English.)
- **Art. 25**: no "rights, titles or benefits" for non-Allied States; "nor shall any right, title or interest of Japan be deemed to be diminished or prejudiced by any provision of the Treaty **in favor of** a State which is not an Allied Power" → China cannot invoke 2(b) as operating in its favour.
- Korea: "benefits", not "titles"; the benefit of a bare renunciation is negative (Japan cannot reassert) and shared by all. Korea has no interest in Taiwan.
- **Art. 23(a)**: "the United States of America as the principal occupying Power".
- **Art. 26** (2nd sentence): "Should Japan make a peace settlement or war claims settlement with any State granting that State greater advantages than those provided by the present Treaty, those same advantages shall be extended to the parties to the present Treaty." MFN aimed at Japan's settlements. If Taipei were a transfer of Taiwan, that "greater advantage" would have to be extended to 48 parties — absurd for exclusive title (VCLT 32) → read Taipei as recognition only. The three-year limit attaches to the 1st sentence's obligation (arguable).

## Treaty of Taipei (P05; English prevails, Art. 14)
- Art. 2 "It is recognized that under Article 2 of the [SFPT] … Japan has renounced all right, title and claim" — recognition, not transfer; nemo dat (renounced 28 Apr, treaty in force 5 Aug).
- Art. 10 "For the purposes of the present Treaty … shall be deemed to include all the inhabitants … who are of the Chinese nationality in accordance with the laws and regulations which have been or may hereafter be enforced by the Republic of China in Taiwan" — purpose-limited fiction; delegates the definition to ROC law (incl. future law); "enforced … in Taiwan" is control language. Structural analogy: US tax law deeming a green-card holder a "U.S. person" without nationality; USCAR laws in Okinawa 1950–72 did not make Ryukyuans US nationals.
- Exchange of Notes No. 1: "under the control of its Government".
- Terminated in Japan's view, 29 Sep 1972 (P57).

## US–ROC MDT 1954 (P08) and Senate (P49)
- Art. VI "For the purposes of Articles II and V …" — functional; US side defined by "jurisdiction", ROC side by island names only; excludes the mainland the ROC then claimed → not a statement of title.
- Senate understanding (committee report, not in the resolution of ratification): no effect on "the legal status or the sovereignty"; Sen. George: "whatever their status may be".

## Japan–PRC Joint Communiqué 1972 (P76) and 1978 Treaty (P77)
- Para. 2 uses 承認 ("recognizes") for the PRC Government; para. 3 does not: 「十分理解し、尊重し」 + 「ポツダム宣言第八項に基づく立場を堅持する」. Object of the verbs: "this **stand**". Potsdam 8: Cairo terms "shall be carried out" (passive; Allies) — after 1952 Japan has nothing to carry out → non-objection, not recognition or transfer.
- Text divergence: zh 「中国政府的这一立场」「坚持遵循」 vs ja 「この中華人民共和国政府の立場」「…に基づく立場を堅持」; no prevailing text → VCLT 33(4).
- Cabinet answers (P79): 2006 — JC 「法的拘束力を有するものではない」; 2022 — asked whether it means 承認, answered only 「十分理解し、尊重する」.
- 1978 Treaty preamble: JC "constitutes the basis" and "the principles enunciated" be strictly observed — anchors principles, not a recognition Japan never made. Art. IV: no effect on relations with third countries.

## UK 1972 (P78)
- Communiqué: "acknowledging the position of the Chinese Government that Taiwan is a province of the PRC" (participial reason for withdrawing the consulate) vs "recognise the Government of the PRC as the sole legal Government of China".
- Adverse: Douglas-Home: "Taiwan should be restored to China. That view has not changed … the Taiwan question is China's internal affair to be settled by the Chinese people themselves." Reply: "should be restored" = not yet restored (Eden 1955); policy changed, not the legal finding. Asked about the wishes of the people of Taiwan: "I do not have any indication".
