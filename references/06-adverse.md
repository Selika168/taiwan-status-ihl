# 06 — Strongest counter-arguments and replies

| Counter-argument | Source | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| Cairo → Potsdam → Surrender chain transferred Taiwan to China | P28, P30, P32 | An undertaking to act is not the act; disposition left to a peace treaty (Pictet p. 275); SFPT 2(b) then named no recipient and Arts. 21/25 excluded China; US (SWNCC 272/1, Truman 1950) and UK (Eden 1955) treated it as unexecuted |
| Treaty of Taipei Art. 4 voided Shimonoseki, so Taiwan reverted | P05 | Executed territorial clauses are not undone by later nullity; Art. 2 separately deals with Taiwan by recognition of renunciation — superfluous if Art. 4 had returned it |
| Japanese Supreme Court 1962: Taiwan "transferred by that treaty" | P53 | Text says only "recognized … has renounced" and "for the purposes … deemed"; Yeh 1952 admitted no attribution; state practice of one party, not binding on third States |
| UK 1972: "should be restored to China … internal affair" | P78 | "should be restored" = not yet restored (Eden 1955); based on both Chinese governments' agreement — a dispute between Chinese governments, not necessarily including the people of the territory; communiqué only "acknowledging" |
| Japan 2006: does not support Taiwan independence, based on JC para. 3 and Potsdam 8 | P79 | Policy, not a finding on title; "not support" ≠ "oppose"; self-determination is not only independence (UNGA 2625); JC "not legally binding" (same answer) |
| US "one China"; Clinton three noes | P85, P84 | "do not support" + "has to be done peacefully"; Bush 2003 opposes unilateral change by **either** side — an outcome reached by consent is not excluded |
| UNGA 2758 settled Taiwan as China | P56 | It decides representation in the UN and expels "the representatives of Chiang Kai-shek"; it does not mention the territory |
| Effective control for decades = title (prescription/acquiescence) | — | Occupant's possession is precarious (entered for the Allies, GO1) and cannot ripen — like a lease (Hong Kong New Territories, 99 years, returned); no prior sovereign to acquiesce (Japan renounced); PRC protests; inhabitants' written reservations = non-acquiescence; Chagos para. 160; not asserted by the ROC itself (03) |
| Tadić substance test: most residents now hold ROC passports, vote, serve | P72 | Service is conscription — barred to an occupant (GC IV Art. 51), so no evidence of allegiance; documents are necessity (Okinawa precedent; Namibia para. 125 [verify]); voting recorded with a written reservation (05). Remaining weak spots: voluntary career service, active use of consular protection; allegiance obtained without any option is not consent |
| The SFPT ended the occupation in 1952 | P04 | Art. 6(a) withdraws forces "from Japan" only; Art. 4(b) treats Art. 2 areas as occupation-era areas; no clause ends Taiwan's occupation (04) |
| After 1952 Taiwan is no High Contracting Party's territory, so GC IV Art. 2(2) cannot apply | P11, P74 | Wall paras 95, 101; the gap is temporal, addressed by routes R1–R5 (05) |
| An ROC–PRC war brings occupation law to Taiwan | — | Not if both are "China": non-international conflict; occupation law depends on Taiwan not being Chinese territory (05) |
| GC IV not in force in 1945–50; Res. 1514 only from 1960 | P11, P73 | Use customary Hague law for 1945–52; "continuing character" (Chagos para. 177) bridges time |
| Taiwan is not on the NSGT list; no administering Member exists; China sits on C-24 | P81, P83 | Listing is not a precondition of self-determination (erga omnes; GA acted without Portugal in Res. 1542); still needs a sponsoring Member |
| UNDRIP Art. 46(1) territorial integrity of sovereign States | P88 | Presupposes settled sovereignty; Taiwan's is undetermined (SFPT 2(b); Eden 1955) |
| Land forfeitures of 1946–47 now have domestic redress (Constitutional Court 2023), so no international issue | P95, P96, P94 | Redress is partial: suits one family at a time, records lost since 1945 bar claims, Nat. Property Act Art. 52-2 is a sale not restitution; partial redress confirms the wrong (ICCPR 2(3)) — see 12 |
| "Rights come from sovereignty/nationality" | — | Post-1945 law detaches core rights from nationality (UDHR 2, 15; ICCPR 2(1)); Arendt's "right to have rights"; if rights depended on recognised sovereignty, whoever controls recognition could declare others rightless |
