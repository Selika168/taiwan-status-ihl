# 03 — Origin and character of the ROC presence in Taiwan

## Entry: acceptance of surrender for the Allies
- General Order No. 1 (P33): Japanese forces in Formosa (and in French Indochina north of 16°N) surrender to Chiang Kai-shek on behalf of the Allied Powers. ROC forces withdrew from northern Indochina in 1946 (Sino-French agreement) — same legal basis, no claim of title there.
- Eden 1955 (P75): taken over "at the direction of the Supreme Commander of the Allied Powers; but this was not a cession, nor did it in itself involve any change of sovereignty. The arrangements made with Chiang Kai-shek put him there on a basis of military occupation pending further arrangements, and did not of themselves constitute the territory Chinese."
- Senator Holland, 1955 (P49): "the Republic of China, now occupying that area".

## Start date
- The occupation began on **2 Sep 1945** with the Instrument of Surrender (P32), which bound "the Emperor, the Japanese Government and their successors" to carry out Potsdam para. 8 ("Japanese sovereignty shall be limited to the islands of Honshu, Hokkaido, Kyushu, Shikoku and such minor islands as we determine"); the Tokyo District Court dated the loss of nationality to the surrender (P50). 25 Oct 1945 is the ROC's own "retrocession" date; adopting it as a critical date silently adopts the ROC's narrative.

## Not prescription
- An occupant's possession is precarious (held for the Allies under GO1), like a lessee's — it cannot ripen into title however long (cf. the New Territories of Hong Kong: leased 1898 for 99 years, returned 1997). Prescription also needs a prior sovereign who acquiesces (none: Japan renounced) and no protest (the PRC protests; inhabitants' recorded reservations count as non-acquiescence). Since 1960 a populated territory cannot be acquired against its people's will (Chagos para. 160). The ROC itself claims retrocession, not prescription. Island of Palmas (1928) criteria [verify].

## Occupation law
- Hague Regulations 1907 (P09) Art. 42 (occupation is factual), Art. 43 (respect the laws in force "unless absolutely prevented"), Art. 45 ("It is forbidden to compel the inhabitants of occupied territory to swear allegiance to the hostile Power."), Arts. 48–49 (occupant may collect taxes).
- **Taxation vs pillage.** Pillage is forbidden (Hague Arts. 28, 47; GC IV Art. 33(2)). Lawful levies meet four tests: legal basis and procedure (Art. 48 "rules of assessment and incidence in force"; Art. 51 written order and receipt); purpose (Art. 49 "only for the needs of the army or of the administration of the territory in question"); destination (Art. 48: occupant "bound to defray the expenses of the administration of the occupied territory to the same extent as the legitimate Government"); owner (Art. 46 "Private property cannot be confiscated"; State property: Art. 53, usufruct Art. 55). Publication alone neither makes a levy lawful nor is its absence the only mark of pillage. Present-day statutory taxation spent on the territory fits Art. 48 — do not advise refusal. Historical items to test [verify facts]: 1945–49 takeover of private Japanese-era property; transfer of the territory's revenue/resources to the mainland civil war ("the territory in question").
- Pictet (1958) Art. 47 p. 275: occupation "deprives the occupied Power of neither its statehood nor its sovereignty … cannot imply any right whatsoever to dispose of territory … A decision on that point can only be reached in the peace treaty. That is a universally recognized rule"; WWII "anticipated annexation" criticised.

## The 1946 nationality order
- Text (P35): 「查台灣人民原係我國國民…其原有我國國籍之人民，自三十四年十月二十五日起，應即一律恢復我國國籍」.
- Literal scope almost empty: the ROC dates from 1912; 1895 inhabitants were Qing subjects; those who stayed after May 1897 elected Japanese nationality (Shimonoseki Art. 5); those born after 1895 were Japanese from birth. Applied in practice to all.
- Option only abroad (P34); UK and Netherlands objected (Tang Shi-yeoung 2005 [secondary]); US Nov 1946 (P02).
- Structurally an occupant's pre-treaty conferral (cf. Golan: UNSC Res. 497 (1981) "null and void").

## Later bases of authority (succeeding each other)
1. 1945–52: military occupation/administration for the Allies.
2. 1952 onward: effective control recognized as control — Taipei Exchange of Notes ("under the control of its Government"), MDT notes ("effectively controls"), Taiwan Relations Act §15(2) "governing authorities on Taiwan" (P58).
3. 1990s onward: consent of the governed (direct elections) — internal legitimacy, not external title.

## Self-description
ROC Constitution, Additional Articles (P86): preamble 「為因應國家統一前之需要」; "free area" (自由地區) vs "mainland area" (Art. 11). A government claiming to be China, administering a territory China never received by treaty — exile in title, administrator in function. UK 1972 (P78): its consul "was never accredited to the Government of Taiwan. He was accredited to the local provincial authorities."
