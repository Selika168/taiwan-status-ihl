## Design notes for a membership body of the people of the territory
- **Critical date: 2 Sep 1945** (Instrument of Surrender; start of occupation), not 25 Oct 1945. Births clause to 28 Apr 1952 (SFPT in force). Include Austronesian members who qualify, with aboriginal-district household records as evidence [verify their form], without determining anyone's indigenous status; catch-all category for residents before the date without a Formosa register and for later arrivals.
- Frame categories as **legal status** (nationality at the start of occupation; GC IV Art. 4; descendants by nationality law), never ethnicity; add "time of arrival" to non-discrimination; never invoke GC IV Art. 49(6) against post-1945 arrivals.
- Local association law may require equal member rights (e.g. Taiwan's Civil Associations Act Art. 16: "會員有表決權、選舉權、被選舉權與罷免權。每一會員為一權"; Art. 14 and 27: expulsion only by the general assembly with 2/3; Art. 26: extraordinary assembly on one day's notice; Art. 8: ≥30 adult founders; former Art. 2 deleted after J.Y. Interpretation No. 644). Do not restrict board eligibility by category; place the claim-specific work (cards, protected-person claim, UN statements) in an internal committee composed of the qualifying members.
- Membership cards: list positively what they attest, and word protected-person status as the holder's **claim** under GC IV Arts. 4 and 47 (see SKILL.md hard rule on emblems). Offer a signed reservation (05, "Daily-life acts are not allegiance").
- Ask the administering authority to declare de facto application of GC IV (route R4 in 05).

## Documenting violations (no individual GC IV complaint mechanism exists)
- Asking the authority whether it implements the Conventions, or an FOI request, tends to yield "in process" — little use. Build a verified evidence file instead, feeding human-rights forums, the ICRC and States.
- Principles: record, never resist (never advise refusing service or tax); first-hand documented facts only; tag each incident on two layers — occupation-law claim (contested) and human-rights law (applies regardless); written consent per use; hash files on intake, keep chain of custody (OHCHR Manual on Human Rights Monitoring; Berkeley Protocol 2022).
- Most documentable ongoing category: compulsory military service of persons claiming protected status (GC IV Art. 51) — each call-up notice is a dated official record. Others: nationality imposed without option (Hague 45, GC IV 47; ICCPR 24(3), 26); measures against persons for asserting status (GC IV 27, 31, 33; ICCPR 19, 21, 22); property (Hague 46, 53; GC IV 53); records (GC IV 47); denial of option; non-domestication and no substitute Protecting Power (GC IV 9, 11, 144).
- Policy ask when domestication comes (external military pressure makes it likely): all four Conventions and the Protocols in full, without carving out the GC IV occupation provisions (Arts. 4, 6(3), 47–78).

## For Austronesian Indigenous Peoples' Organizations (IPOs)
- PFII: IPOs need **no** ECOSOC status; first-time accreditation via the iCSO form (mission, structure, focal point), then session registration in Indico; pre-registration typically Jan–Feb, closing 2–4 weeks before the April session (social.desa.un.org).
- Also EMRIP, UN Voluntary Fund for Indigenous Peoples, SR on the rights of Indigenous Peoples.
- Must be composed of and governed by indigenous peoples; others may assist only.

## Non-Self-Governing Territory (NSGT) listing
- Charter Art. 73 binds Members "which have or assume responsibilities for the administration" — none currently administers Taiwan (ROC not a Member since 1971; PRC does not administer).
- GA can decide over the administering State's objection: Res. 1542 (1960) on Portugal (P81). Criteria: Res. 1541 Principles IV–V ("geographically separate and … distinct ethnically and/or culturally from the country administering it"; "arbitrarily … in a position or status of subordination"); Principle VI full self-government = independence / free association / integration.
- Reinscription practice (French Polynesia, Res. 67/265, P82; New Caledonia, Res. 41/41): territory's own institutions request → regional/political group support → Member States table draft → GA "recognizes … remains a Non-Self-Governing Territory".
- C-24 hears petitioners only on agenda items (listed territories + Puerto Rico by its own decisions) (P83); its 29 members include China and Russia.

## Precedents for recognition gatekeeping
- Palestine: 1989 accession request — Swiss depositary "not … in a position to decide"; 1982 unilateral undertaking to apply GC IV accepted as valid; 2014 accession accepted after UNGA 67/19 (2012) (Wall para. 91, P74). ICC: 2009 Art. 12(3) declaration not acted on; 2015 accepted.
- ICJ Statute Art. 36(2) declarations are open only to parties to the Statute.
- The ICRC: an NGO's declaration produces no Convention effect; Protecting Powers are designated by the Parties (GC IV Art. 9), and when none functions the **Occupying/Detaining Power** must request a substitute (Art. 11) — the population need not apply.
