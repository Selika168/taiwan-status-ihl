# 12 — Land registration 1945–1950: private property under occupation law

The clearest documented case of an occupant's measure that took **private property** of the inhabitants is the post-1945 general land registration. This file sets out what the record shows, how occupation law and human-rights law apply, the ROC's own later findings, and the limits of the argument.

Sources: P09 (Hague Regulations), P11 (GC IV), P93 (Changhua Land Office booklet), P94 (Control Yuan report 2022), P95 (Constitutional Court 112-Hsien-Pan-20, 2023), P96 (Tainan High Court 2024).

## 2. What changed after the surrender (1945–1950)

| Date | Measure | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1946-04-05 | Governor-General's Office (行政長官公署) notice 卯微卅五署民字第2896號: all holders of rights in land, public or private, to declare to the local land-adjustment office (土地整理處) with documents | **Declaration window: 21 April – 20 May 1946 (one month)**; any one of four documents sufficed (registration certificate, ledger copy, three years' land-tax receipts, other proof) | P94; P93 |
| 1946-04-29 | Land Act (1930) as amended, Art. 57 | Land not declared within the deadline "deemed ownerless", announced, then registered as State land. The Control Yuan found this rule was designed for clearing land on the mainland, where no registration existed, and was carried into Taiwan, where it did | P94 |
| 1946-11-26 / 12-03 | Executive Yuan 臺灣地籍釐整辦法, Art. 4 | In areas registered under the Japanese government, right-holders must surrender their registration certificates to the land authority for verification, announcement and exchange for ROC title deeds | P94 |
| 1947-05-02 | 臺灣省土地權利憑證繳驗及換發權利書狀辦法, **Art. 14** | 「逾期無人申請驗證之土地，或經申請而逾限未補繳證件者，其土地視為無主土地，由該管縣市地政機關公告2個月，公告期滿，無人提出異議者，即為國有土地。」 | P94 |
| 1947-07-10 | Executive Yuan order | Custody by the land authority; State registration two years after announcement | P94 |
| from 1947-07 | New title deeds issued; registry now the **administrative land authority** (地政機關 / 地政事務所), not the courts | change of registry institution | P93 |
| 1973-04-03 | Executive Yuan principle on land not declared in time, item 1 | Land already registered as State land after the ownerless-land procedure: no later claims or late registration accepted | P94 |

Context recorded by the ROC's own organs: untrained and too few staff, language barriers ("本縣高山族同胞，幾佔半數，既不通臺語，更不諳漢文" — Taitung office, June 1946), fast-changing rules, and the period of the 1947 228 Incident (P94). A one-month window in a population that read Japanese, not Chinese, was the gateway to forfeiture.

## 3. Occupation-law analysis

The measures fall inside the occupation that began on 2 Sep 1945 (Instrument of Surrender) and, in any case, before the state of war ended on 28 Apr 1952 (SFPT Art. 1(a)) — see `03-roc-presence.md`.

1. **Hague Regulations Art. 46 — the main ground.** "Family honour and rights, the lives of persons, and **private property** … must be respected. **Private property cannot be confiscated.**" (P09). Registering privately owned, already-registered land as State land because the owner missed a one-month declaration is a transfer of private property to the occupant's State without compensation — confiscation in substance, whatever the procedural label.
2. **Art. 55** — the occupant is "only … administrator and usufructuary" of the *hostile State's* real estate (P09). Even Japanese public land did not become the occupant's property; *a fortiori* private land cannot be made the occupant's State land.
3. **Art. 43** — the occupant must respect, "unless absolutely prevented, the laws in force in the country" (P09). Two changes are tested here: (a) the registry moved from the courts to an administrative authority; (b) the law of title changed so that a valid registered title could be lost by non-declaration. Restoring an orderly register after the war may be a legitimate administrative need, so (a) is the weaker point; (b) is not necessary for order and fails the "absolutely prevented" test. Lead with Art. 46, use Art. 43 in support.
4. **GC IV Art. 53** (destruction of private property) does not fit — nothing was destroyed. GC IV applies from 1950 at the earliest for the ROC/PRC chain (`05-human-rights-bases.md`); the 1946–47 measures are judged by the Hague Regulations as customary law (Nuremberg).
5. **Human-rights layer** (applies regardless of status): UDHR Art. 17(2) "No one shall be arbitrarily deprived of his property"; ICCPR Art. 26 (equal protection), Art. 2(3) (effective remedy). ICCPR/ICESCR are domesticated in ROC law since 2009.

## 4. What the ROC itself has since found

| Organ | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional Court, 2023-12-29 | Owners of Japanese-era land registered as State land for missing the deadline may sue to cancel the State registration **without any limitation period**; Supreme Court precedent 70-Tai-Shang-311 unconstitutional in that part (Constitution Art. 15). The general registration 「原則上承認人民日治時期之土地所有權」; Land Act Art. 43 「依本法所為之登記，有絕對效力」 protects only third parties relying on the register and does not take the true owner's right | P95 |
| Tainan High Court, 2024-02-21 | State registration of 1950 cancelled; title restored to a lineage trust on the strength of a 1946 ledger copy; the general registration 「並無使不動產物權發生變動之意……該登記與物權之歸屬無關」 | P96 |
| Control Yuan, 2022 | Refusing restoration under the 1947 Art. 14 and the 1973 principle 「甚難通過『憲法』保障財產權之檢驗」; later remedies prove the 1946–47 verification 「問題重重，確有疏失」 | P94 |
| Legislature | 地籍清理條例 (2007, in force 2008); 國有財產法 Art. 52-2 (2000): occupiers since before 1946-12-31 may **buy** the State-registered land at the first announced value | P94 |

These findings are strong evidence that the taking was wrongful. Note the gap: Art. 52-2 makes the owner **pay** for land that was theirs; court redress requires each family to sue with Japanese-era proof; records lost since 1945 cannot be sued on.

## 5. Counter-arguments and replies

| Counter-argument | Reply |
|---|---|
| "Registration was a neutral administrative clean-up; owners had notice and a deadline." | The ROC's own Constitutional Court calls it a records procedure that did not change ownership (P95) — so the transfer to the State had no legal basis in ownership; Control Yuan found the procedure defective (P94). Under Art. 46 a deadline does not license confiscation. |
| "The courts now give redress, so there is a domestic remedy and nothing for international bodies." | Redress is partial: no limitation period only since 2023; Art. 52-2 is a sale, not restitution; lost records bar claims. Partial domestic redress is evidence of the wrong and of incomplete remedy (ICCPR 2(3)). |
| "Land Act Art. 43 gives registration absolute effect." | Only for third parties relying on the register (P95, P96). Against the State as first registrant it does not apply. |
| "Taiwan was Chinese territory from 25 Oct 1945, so ROC land law simply applied." | The occupation began on 2 Sep 1945; no instrument transferred title (`01`, `04`); even on the ROC's own view its courts now restore Japanese-era titles. |

## 6. Rules for use

- Lead with **Hague Art. 46** and the ROC's own findings (P94, P95, P96). Use Art. 43 in support.
- State the one-month window, Art. 14 and the 1973 bar with dates; quote, do not paraphrase.
- On 1947 violence, name individuals (e.g. High Court judge 吳鴻麒; lawyers 李瑞漢, 林桂端, 林連宗) [verify against the 228 Memorial Foundation records]; never state that "the judges were killed" as a class.
- For individual cases: Japanese-era registration book (登記簿), land ledger (土地臺帳), the post-1946 first-registration entry, parcel-number history (地號沿革), any refusal letter. The same Japanese-era records prove a person's pre-1945 status (`05`, `08`).
- Do not advise occupying or refusing to vacate land; advise the domestic suit under P95 first, then documentation for UN mechanisms (`07-un-channels.md`).
