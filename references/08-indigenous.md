# 08 — Indigenous peoples vs the people of the territory

## Two categories, two kinds of proof
| Category | Who | Proof | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| People of the territory | inhabitants with Japanese nationality via a Formosan/Pescadores register before 25 Oct 1945, and descendants (all ethnic groups) | pre-1945 household register / nationality | option of nationality; self-determination (Chagos para. 160 "the people of the territory concerned") |
| Indigenous peoples | Austronesian peoples — mountain peoples **and** plains (Pingpu) peoples | register ethnicity column 「生」→「高砂」, 「熟」→「平」; oral genealogy; community attestation; self-identification + acceptance | UNDRIP; PFII; EMRIP |
One person can be both. Han-origin islanders (register 「福」 福建系 / 「廣」 廣東系; also 「漢」) are people of the territory but not indigenous under the UN standard — they are, relative to Austronesian peoples, later settlers (17th–19th c.). Original nationality cannot distinguish indigenous peoples: all 本島人 held the same Japanese nationality.

## Not only mountain peoples
- Constitutional Court 111-Hsien-Pan-17 (2022) (P87), holding 1: protected indigenous peoples "include all Austronesian peoples existing in Taiwan"; para. [14]: Japanese-era registers classified 「生」「高砂」 vs 「熟」「平」 by region and degree of assimilation; holding 3: fallback for persons whose register shows 「熟」 or 「平」.
- Special law for Pingpu peoples passed 17 Oct 2025, promulgated 23 Oct 2025 [news]; Siraya approved 2026 [news, verify].
- Basic Law Art. 2(1) (P90): self-identification + Executive Yuan approval.

## Register entries are not ancestry proofs
- The ethnicity column was filled by the **father's line** (by the mother's only if unknown) [Kaohsiung household-registration guidance] — maternal Pingpu ancestry and assimilated Pingpu families recorded as 「福」 are invisible.
- Qing-era genealogy attachment and Han surnames given to 熟番 are plausible mechanisms [verify specifics]; but "85% of Taiwanese carry indigenous genes" (Lin Marie) is contested (Chen Shu-juo, Duan Hong-kun), and Japanese-era registers show Han–Pingpu intermarriage under 1% (Yeh Kao-hwa) [secondary].
- Nationality ≠ ancestry: Qing subjects were not ROC nationals; pre-1945 nationality proves membership of the people of the territory regardless of origin.

## International standard is wider than Taiwan's
- Cobo study (P89) para. 379: "historical continuity with pre-invasion and pre-colonial societies"; para. 380: continuity "may consist of … **one or more** of" ancestral lands, **common ancestry**, culture, language, residence; paras 381–382: self-identification + acceptance; the community's "sovereign right and power to decide who belongs to them".
- UNDRIP (P88) Arts. 3, 8 (no forced assimilation; redress), 33(1); Art. 46(1) limit (see 06-adverse).
- Taiwan's ruling para. [36] excludes peoples "fully assimilated" — criticisable under UNDRIP Art. 8 (it penalises the victims of assimilation).
- ILO C169 Art. 1 (descent at conquest/colonisation; self-identification "fundamental criterion") [verify text].

## Practice
- Only indigenous peoples can found IPOs (FPIC, UNDRIP Arts. 18–19); partners may assist without speaking for them.
- Sensitivities: for many indigenous activists every regime — Qing, Japan (理蕃 policy; 1930 Musha), ROC — was external; do not present a pre-1945-nationality narrative as their liberation. The PRC voted for UNDRIP but says there are no indigenous peoples in China and classifies Taiwan's as 高山族 (IWGIA 2025).
