# 09 — Routes tested and found not to work (do not recommend)

These routes have been proposed in public debate or attempted by civil-society groups. Each failed for the reason given. The underlying intuitions (occupation, nationality, protected persons) survive and are better argued as in 05-human-rights-bases.md.

| Route | Why it fails | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A citizens' group declares adherence to / accession to the Geneva Conventions | An NGO's declaration produces no Convention effect (ICRC); accession is for "Powers" | GC IV Art. 155; ICRC reply |
| Asking Japan to act as Protecting Power for the inhabitants | Japan regards them as non-nationals since 1952; Protecting Powers are designated by Parties; substitutes are the occupant's duty (GC IV Arts. 9, 11) | 07 |
| Refusing taxes to the current administration | Hague Regs. 48–49 permit an occupant to tax; no legal effect; legal risk | 03 |
| "Colonies can be ceded but national territory cannot" | Not positive law (Alsace-Lorraine, Alaska, Schleswig, German eastern territories); would also make Korea's separation unlawful | — |
| "Korea, receiving the benefits of SFPT Art. 2, is the lawful government of Taiwan" | Art. 21 grants benefits, not titles; Art. 2(a) concerns Korea itself; absurd under VCLT 32 | 04 |
| "The US holds temporary de jure sovereignty as principal occupying Power" (*Lin v. US*) | Courts treated status as a political question; the SFPT names the US principal occupying Power "but does not indicate over what" | P60, P61 |
| "Rights come only from sovereignty/nationality" | Leaves post-1949 arrivals and the stateless rightless; contradicts UDHR 2, 15 and ICCPR 2(1) | 06 |
| A citizens' group proclaims itself a Red Cross society, adopts a red-crystal (or red-cross) emblem with an inserted national or flag symbol, and marks vehicles or premises with it | National societies are recognised by the ICRC, one per State, on recognition by their own government; a sign inside the red crystal is for recognised national societies (AP III Art. 3); unauthorised use is misuse of the emblem and may be penalised domestically; a national or military flag is not a protective sign. Anecdotal restraint by military aircraft is not evidence of legal recognition | GC I Arts. 44, 53; AP III Arts. 2–3, 6; Statutes of the Movement Art. 4 [verify] |
| Presenting an iCSO registration as "UN recognition" | iCSO is an open directory | 07 |
