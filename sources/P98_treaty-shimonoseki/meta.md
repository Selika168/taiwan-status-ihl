Signed at Shimonoseki 1895-04-17; ratifications exchanged at Chefoo 1895-05-08 (Art. 11), so the two-year period of Art. 5 ran to 1897-05-08.
Art. 2(b) cedes "the island of Formosa, together with all islands appertaining or belonging to the said island of Formosa"; Art. 2(c) the Pescadores, defined by coordinates (119–120° E, 23–24° N). The cession of southern Fengtien (Art. 2(a)) was reversed by the Liaotung Convention of 1895-11-08, which also suppressed Art. 3.
Art. 5: inhabitants wishing to live outside the ceded districts may sell their real property and leave within two years; those who have not left "shall, at the option of Japan, be deemed to be Japanese subjects".
Wording differs between the texts: Art. 2 cedes "in perpetuity and full sovereignty" (en) / 「土地ノ主權…永遠日本國ニ割與ス」 (ja) / 「管理下開地方之權…永遠讓與日本」 (zh); Art. 5 "at the option of Japan" (en) / 「日本國ノ都合ニヨリ…視爲スコトアルヘシ」 (ja) / 「酌宜視為日本臣民」 (zh). The Separate Article signed the same day (議定專條 §§1–2, zh text) provides that the English text is to govern any dispute between the Chinese and Japanese texts.
The 1917 Customs edition is a reprint, not the signed original; the Japanese text is a Wikisource transcription of the official gazette and has not been compared with the NDL scan; the Chinese text is a Wikisource transcription with no stated base text.
