# Taiwan status, occupation law and human rights

This skill condenses a source-checked analysis. Every claim below is backed by a primary text in `sources/` (folder `Pxx_*`, with `meta.md` giving URL and sha256). Quote from those texts, not from memory. Items marked **[verify]** rest on secondary sources or recollection.

## Five conclusions (state them in this order)

1. **Empire of Japan → State of Japan: no transfer of sovereignty to anyone else.** One continuing State; only the *internal* holder of sovereignty changed (Emperor → people; Miyazawa's "August Revolution" theory). The SFPT presupposes continuity (Arts. 1(a), 2, 18(b) "affirms", 19(d) validation of occupation acts). "Void for coercion" fails (VCLT 52 covers only force "in violation of the principles … of the Charter"; Art. 4 non-retroactivity; Art. 75 aggressor clause; ILC 1966 commentary). "A new State under occupation" fails (Pictet 1958 p. 273: forming new entities under occupation is prohibited). → `references/02-japan-continuity.md`
2. **Formosa and the Pescadores: renounced, but transferred to no one.** SFPT Art. 2(b) names no recipient (contrast 2(a) Korea). Treaty of Taipei Art. 2 only *recognizes* the renunciation; Japan could not on 5 Aug 1952 transfer what it renounced on 28 Apr 1952. Every later instrument avoids title (Taipei Art. 10 "for the purposes … deemed"; MDT Art. VI "for the purposes of Articles II and V"; Senate understanding; Eden 1955 "uncertain or undetermined"; JC 1972 "understands and respects", not "recognizes"). → `references/01-sovereignty-chain.md`
3. **The ROC entered as an occupant acting for the Allies.** General Order No. 1; Eden 1955: "at the direction of the Supreme Commander … on a basis of military occupation pending further arrangements". Occupation transfers no sovereignty and cannot dispose of territory (Hague Regs. Art. 43; Pictet p. 275). The occupation began on 2 Sep 1945 (Instrument of Surrender), not on the ROC's "retrocession" date of 25 Oct 1945. 1945 was **surrender, not the end of the war**: the state of war ended only on 28 Apr 1952, when the SFPT entered into force (Art. 1(a)). No SFPT clause ends it (Art. 6(a) withdraws occupation forces "from Japan" only), and an occupant's possession cannot ripen into title by prescription. The 1946 collective nationality order was an occupant's pre-treaty act (UK and Netherlands objected). → `references/03-roc-presence.md`
4. **Under the SFPT, China (ROC or PRC) and Korea acquire nothing over Taiwan.** Art. 21 gives China only the *benefits* of Arts. 10 and 14(a)2; Art. 25: no "rights, titles or benefits" to non-Allied States, and no Japanese right diminished "in favor of" them; Art. 26 MFN makes any greater advantage given to one State shareable by all parties — impossible for exclusive title. Korea: "benefits", not title. UNGA 2758 concerns UN representation only. → `references/04-sfpt.md`
5. **Sovereignty was disposed of; human rights remain.** The right-holders are the people of the territory (pre-1945 inhabitants and descendants). Bases: occupation law (Hague 43, 45; GC IV 47 + 6(3); Wall paras 95, 101 against the "missing reversioner"); nationality and human-rights law (no option ever given: 1895 yes / 1946 no / 1952 no; 1952 circular contrary to Const. of Japan Art. 10 per Iwasawa; Tadić substance test); self-determination (Chagos paras 160, 172, 177, 180; UNGA 2625); indigenous rights for Austronesian peoples (UNDRIP; Cobo). No challenge to the SFPT is needed — the SFPT is the best evidence. → `references/05-human-rights-bases.md`

## Workflow

0. **Read `references/11-model-priors.md` first.** It lists conclusions a model tends to reach from background knowledge that the record overturns (retrocession date, prescription, "the SFPT ended the occupation", "GC IV cannot apply", passports/voting as allegiance, etc.). Do not re-derive them from priors.
1. Identify which of the five questions is asked; answer with the conclusion first, then the evidence (quote + `Pxx`).
2. Always state the **strongest counter-argument** and the reply (`references/06-adverse.md`): UK 1972 "should be restored … internal affair"; Japan 2006 "does not support independence"; Cairo/Potsdam/Surrender chain; Japanese Supreme Court 1962 "transferred"; Tadić cuts both ways; timing (GC IV from 1950; Res. 1514 from 1960); UNDRIP Art. 46(1).
3. Distinguish **"people of the territory"** (proved by pre-1945 nationality / Taiwan household register — for option and self-determination) from **"indigenous peoples"** (Austronesian descent; Japanese-era register ethnicity column 生/高砂, 熟/平; self-identification + community acceptance). Original nationality cannot distinguish indigenous peoples. → `references/08-indigenous.md`
4. For action questions, use `references/07-un-channels.md` (Special Procedures need no status; ECOSOC status then PFII as NGO; IPOs need no ECOSOC status; NSGT listing obstacles; ICJ advisory opinion via a Member State).
5. Before proposing any route, check `references/09-dead-ends.md`.
6. Mark anything not in `sources/` as **[verify]**; the open list is `references/10-verify.md`.

## Hard rules

- Dates: **2 Sep 1945 = surrender** (start of occupation); **28 Apr 1952 = end of the state of war** (SFPT Art. 1(a)). Never call 1945 "the end of the war"; "post-war" means after 1952.
- Never claim a treaty transferred Taiwan to the ROC or PRC; never claim Taiwan still belongs to Japan or to Korea.
- Never present an NGO declaration, a self-proclaimed government, or an iCSO registration as UN recognition.
- Do not advise refusing taxes (no legal effect; personal legal risk).
- "Not support independence" ≠ "oppose" ≠ prohibition (Clinton 1998; Bush 2003 opposes *unilateral change by either side*).
- Original nationality ≠ ancestry: a register entry 「福」/「廣」 is a father's-line administrative classification, not proof of Chinese origin or nationality.
- Cards, flags and documents of civil-society groups: list positively what they attest (membership, category, evidence source, the holder's *claim* of protected-person status). Never put the red cross, red crescent, red crystal or other distinctive signs protected by the Geneva Conventions, Additional Protocols or the 1954 Hague Convention (blue shield) on them. If a user proposes to use such an emblem, warn before drafting: use is reserved to the medical services, the components of the Movement and protected sites (GC I Arts. 38, 44, 53; AP I Arts. 18, 38, 66); misuse may be penalised under domestic law and, in armed conflict, perfidious use is prohibited (AP I Art. 37). A national or military flag (any State's) is not a protective sign under these Conventions.
- Keep personal data out: never put names, household-register numbers or private correspondence into outputs.
